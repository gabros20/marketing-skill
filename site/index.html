/marketing

marketing says what the product is and who it's for, in words a buyer believes — then creates, captures and attributes demand for it. The launch. Being found, in search, in an AI answer, in an app store, in someone else's newsletter. The page that converts the visit, the email that arrives, the ad budget and whether it did anything. What makes it different is that it grades its own evidence: marketing advice mixes real, replicated findings with folklore repeated until it sounded measured — so every claim here says how solid it is and where it stops applying. The default reader is one person building a digital product, with a small budget and no analytics team — which changes the answers, not just their scale.

Route before acting. One job, at most one base surface, overlays added only when they apply. If the request names no business model, the skill does not stall — it assumes self-serve SaaS and says which it assumed.

The router is the skill. There is no fixed campaign pipeline to run start-to-finish — each job stands alone and enters where your request is. The animation traces one path (the flagship, deciding what a measurement can honestly support); the sections below map the whole surface it routes across.

Position the product, then create, capture and attribute demand

The scope is the full external-demand surface — positioning, audience, launch, SEO, AI search, content, landing pages, email and lifecycle, paid, social and community, partnerships and PR, app store optimization, and measurement — across every place demand actually happens: search and AI answer engines, inboxes, ad platforms, app stores, launch platforms, integration marketplaces. Those platforms are the terrain, never the subject — the techniques are the same; the surfaces say how each one reshapes them. marketing writes copy, requirements, briefs, plans and measurement specs. It writes no production code, and it does not compose the page it briefs.

The default reader is one person, with a small budget and no analytics team. That is not a simplification of enterprise practice — it changes the answers. Platform attribution over-claims by construction, a valid lift test needs scale almost nobody has, and marketing-mix modelling needs years of weekly data. What remains at that scale is real, and this pack teaches it instead of pretending the enterprise toolkit scales down.

Attribution and measurement — the flagship

Ask an agent whether a campaign worked and it reads the number the platform reported. Nobody checked whether those conversions were incremental — whether they would have happened anyway. That question is the one the field mostly avoids, and answering it honestly is this pack's flagship job. Platform-reported conversions answer "what fired last," never "what was incremental." The gap between the two is not a rounding error. In eBay's own large-scale paid-search field experiment, naive estimation put ROI above +4,100% uncontrolled and above +1,400% with time and geo controls; switching the ads off in randomized markets measured −63% ROI (95% CI [−124%, −3%]) — a sign flip, not a margin. The mechanism generalizes past search ads: people clicking high-intent ads were mostly going to buy anyway, which is why retargeting is the format most likely to overstate its own incrementality.

The three rigorous options are gated by scale — not by budget, and not by tooling

Why almost nobody can run a valid lift test

  • the findingLewis & Rao (QJE 130(4), 2015) analyzed 25 large field experiments: an informative advertising experiment "can easily require more than 10 million person-weeks"
  • the scale gapthe median campaign in that dataset would need to be 9× larger to distinguish a genuine +50% ROI from breakeven, and 62× larger to resolve a 10% ROI difference
  • the consequencethis is a statistical-power problem baked into how noisy sales data is — not a tooling problem, and not one more spend solves. Most advertisers, including most large ones, never reach it

MMM has the same shape of gate from the other direction: Robyn (Meta, MIT) documents roughly two years of weekly data at a 1:10 variable-to-observation ratio; Meridian (Google, Apache-2.0) says two years for a geo-level model is "too low to estimate the model reliably" and recommends three. Neither names a dollar threshold — the gate is data history, which is why MMM is unreachable for an early-stage product regardless of ad spend.

What makes this different

Almost every individual fact in this pack exists somewhere else. What does not exist anywhere else is a pack that grades those facts and connects them to each other — so the wedge is named as adjudication and integration, not discovery.

Every claim carries its tier and its boundary

The canon mixes decades-replicated law, self-selected award-entry corpora, and arithmetic heuristics with a disclaimer attached. They are not cited the same way here.

  • (a) well-evidenced in its own domain — decades of replication on consumer repeat-purchase panels, many countries, peer-reviewed
  • (b) plausibly transferable, thinner — real B2B replications exist and the direction holds, but smaller and younger; no B2B SaaS or subscription dataset has been located in any open-access institute publication
  • (c) folklore extrapolation — applying (a) or (b) to a zero-audience, pre-launch, n=1 product. Every dataset behind these laws needs an existing, measurable buyer population
  • the boundary that matters most — a pre-launch founder sits outside every dataset surveyed in both directions: no evidence for, none against. That is the honest answer, not a gap to paper over

Volatile facts carry a date and the check that re-verifies them

Store limits, spam thresholds, crawler policies, supported rich results and platform guidelines all change with no deploy on your side. The pack teaches the check, not the list — and it never names a number in order to forbid it.

  • four SEO myths, killed with dates — AI authorship is not penalized (the test is purpose, not authorship); there is no separate helpful-content algorithm since it folded into core ranking; a rater's score cannot move a page; E-E-A-T is not four co-equal factors — Trust is the center
  • llms.txt is falsified, not merely unproven — by four independent, non-citing evidence classes, including the spec's own text and every major provider's crawler documentation. Ship it as cheap, low-risk and of unverified payoff — never as a tactic
  • the self-verifying alternative — blocking or allowing named AI crawlers by user-agent needs no trust in an unobserved consumption claim: user-agents are directly observable in your own logs
  • specimen vs. evidence — a measurement vendor's aggregate is evidence about a pattern and a specimen of the genre that sells the service. The interest gets named, including when it makes the claim stronger
What it will not do: assert a number it could not verify. No ESOV coefficient is shipped, not even hedged, because none was located at a primary source. The 60/40 brand-versus-activation split is labelled as what it is — an IPA Databank figure from a self-submitted awards corpus, and a B2C one. The Thomaz dispute over mental and physical availability is presented, not settled. An honest "nobody has measured this, and here is why" is a deliverable, not a failure to produce one.

The universal invariants

These govern every route, whichever references it loads.

  • Grade the claim before you use itEvery empirical claim carries its evidence tier and the population it was measured on. Replicated law, award-entry corpora and arithmetic heuristics get cited differently.
  • Name the domain boundary, especially when it excludes your readerSay a dataset doesn't cover you instead of borrowing a panel's confidence.
  • Platform-reported is not incrementalA dashboard number answers "what fired last," never "would this have happened anyway." The two are never reported as the same fact.
  • Date-stamp every volatile fact, and ship the checkTeach the check, not the list — limits, thresholds, crawler policies and platform guidelines change with no deploy on your side.
  • Specimen vs. evidenceName the interest of anyone selling what they measure — including when it makes their claim stronger.
  • Never name a number in order to forbid itA figure that failed verification is not repeated with a disclaimer attached. State the mechanism and point at the primary source.
  • Segment by jurisdiction before any sendA single global list treated uniformly is unlawful in at least one regime it touches. The consent basis is decided before the copy is written.
  • The mechanism is fine; verify the claim underneath itScarcity, anchoring, framing and social proof are ethical or dark patterns depending only on whether the underlying claim is true.
  • State what you could not verifyAn honest "nobody has measured this, and here is why" is a deliverable, not a failure to produce one — and it is the answer nobody else in the genre gives.

What a pass produces

Copy is delivered as copy. Requirements are delivered as requirements. Neither is delivered as production code. A marketing artifact is incomplete unless it carries the audience and buying situation it addresses, the one goal, every claim with a named proof source, the evidence tier and domain boundary of any law it cites, an as-of date on every volatile fact, the consent basis if anything gets sent, the measurement method and what that method cannot answer, and the sibling packs it hands to.
